PHASE 17 — THE IPO HOTSPOT
==========================
User question: "imagine the SPAC IPO, we would not have been able to process it,
we need to be able to scale out tickers with large volumes."

Measured the hot-key problem at the PRODUCER, which is where it binds first:
key=symbol sends a symbol to one partition and therefore one leader broker, so
every producer writing it queues behind the same leader.

  uniform feed,   key=symbol    873,333 prices/s
  90% one ticker, key=symbol    293,333 prices/s   -66%
  90% one ticker, key=salted    764,444 prices/s   -12%
  90% one ticker, key=adaptive  788,888 prices/s   -10%, ordering kept for cold names

Adaptive keying (salt only symbols above a share threshold) beat blanket salting
AND preserved per-symbol ordering for quiet names. Clean runs later showed keying
helps INGEST only -- processing was unchanged (23,807 vs 23,603 rec/s), because
spreading the write cannot widen a stage whose key space is the business domain.

WHY SALTING DOES NOT FAN OUT ON CONFLUENT — IT IS TOO LATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The generator writes price.symbol as the Kafka key (DataGenerator.java:185)
and `prices` is DISTRIBUTED BY HASH(`key`) INTO 48 BUCKETS. Ten symbols hash
into AT MOST 10 buckets; 38+ are permanently empty (the same fact that stalled
the watermarks).

=> The SOURCE can only read ~10-way no matter how many buckets or CFUs exist.
   Query-time salting widens the CONFLATION OPERATOR to 80 keys but runs AFTER
   the source read. A downstream PARTITION BY cannot widen a source.

FIX (untested): salt at WRITE time -- producer keys records `symbol|salt` so
they physically spread across all 48 buckets, then reduce back to `symbol` in
the query. That is a producer change, not a query change.

WHY AWS STILL GAINED 2.66x FROM THE SAME "TOO LATE" SALT: LocalPriceConflator's
keyBy forces a NETWORK SHUFFLE, redistributing records across all 20 subtasks
regardless of arrival partition. The source stays 10-way but the expensive
parse/conflate work fans out. Confluent's planner did not turn the salted CTE
into equivalent extra parallelism -- the pool settled at <=10 CFU.

WITHDRAW THE cap-10 vs cap-20 COST COMPARISON
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  cap-10  44.4M / 1127s, 9.61 CFU avg -> 4.06 CFU-min per million records
  cap-20  79.9M /  882s, 7.64 CFU avg -> 1.41 CFU-min per million records
Two runs of the SAME workload at effectively the SAME compute (both peaked at
10 CFU) differ 2.9x in cost per record. That is the ramp-up + backlog-size
artifact, not economics. The rec/s-per-$ figures for these rungs are NOT
comparable and are withdrawn.

WHAT SURVIVES: both pools peaked at 10 CFU, so cap-20 bought nothing; and
because Confluent bills consumption rather than cap, over-provisioning the cap
costs nothing. That is the defensible utilisation finding.
