backpressure from the MV operator propagates upstream through the SHARED
position operator into the order path: a price storm can slow trades,
violating the Case-1 guarantee. The "no windows" purity becomes a liability
exactly here.

Wrote the Phase 7 plan into PLAN.md (review-gated, same lifecycle as before):

measure MV emission rate, MV busy time, backpressure, and the key number —
order-path latency. Find the knee.

Step 2 — Fix with a deliberate window: conflated re-valuation.

RESPONSE (full plan-review message, verbatim)
---------------------------------------------
The Phase 7 plan is written, recorded as working prompt 7 in
prompts/phase7_prompt.txt, added to PLAN.md, and pushed (48d4243). Here it
is for your review:

Why your theory is right (worth stating precisely, since it's the heart of
the phase): the MV joins emit one record per holder on every price tick —
O(holders) work per tick. But the sharper problem is where the backpressure
goes: when the MV operator saturates, Flink backpressure propagates upstream
to BOTH of its inputs, and one of them is the position operator that also
feeds the order path. So a price storm doesn't just delay market values — it
can stall trades, breaking the Case-1 guarantee we demonstrated in Phase 6.
Today's demo numbers hid this because fan-out was tiny (5 accounts) and
prices slow (20/s).

The plan, three review-gated steps:

1. Prove it (config-only). Make fan-out realistic — 50 accounts — hold
   trades at 1000/s, and storm prices at 10x (200/s) then 100x (2,000/s).
   Measure MV emission rate, MV busy time, backpressure, and the number that
   matters: order-path latency. Find the knee where it degrades.

2. Fix with a deliberate window: conflated re-valuation. Position-driven MV
   stays immediate — the order path never waits. Price-driven re-valuation
   moves to a per-ticker processing-time timer (mv.reval.interval.ms, e.g.
   250 ms; 0 restores today's behavior): store the latest price, re-value
   all holders once per interval at the freshest price, absorbing
   intermediate ticks. That caps price-driven work at
   holders x (1000/interval) per ticker/sec no matter how fast prices tick.
   Crucially, final state after quiesce is still position x latest price, so
   the entire Phase 4 validation suite must pass unchanged. A new
   demoTicksConflated metric will show the absorption live on the dashboard.

3. Verify. Harness tests with controlled processing time (N ticks in an
   interval -> exactly one emission at the last price; interval=0 identical
   to old behavior), re-run the same storms expecting flat order latency at
   100x prices, full make test + make validate green, results appended to
   PERF_RESULTS.md.

One design choice you may want to weigh in on: I picked processing-time
timers for the conflation interval (simple, load-shedding is inherently a
wall-clock concern) rather than event-time. That's the standard choice for
conflation, but flag it if you disagree.
